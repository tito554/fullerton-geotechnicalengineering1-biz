In Fullerton, geophysics plays a critical role in characterizing the complex Quaternary alluvium and shallow groundwater conditions typical of the Los Angeles Basin. Our non-invasive surveys help engineers comply with California Building Code Chapter 16 and ASCE 7 site classification requirements without excessive borings. We routinely apply the [HVSR microtremor survey](hvsr-microtremor-survey) to map fundamental site periods and constrain the depth to high-velocity bedrock, delivering reliable Vs profiles for seismic design in accordance with local ordinances.

Major infrastructure and commercial developments across Orange County rely on these methods for accurate subsurface models. When projects demand detailed cross-sections through fault zones or paleochannels, our [seismic tomography](refraction-reflection) resolves P-wave and S-wave velocity gradients essential for excavation planning and foundation analysis. Whether supporting a high-rise in downtown Fullerton or a critical utility corridor, combining passive and active source techniques ensures comprehensive ground characterization from the near surface down to engineering bedrock.

Geophysical services in Fullerton provide a non-intrusive window into subsurface conditions, essential for addressing the engineering challenges posed by the local geology. The area is underlain by complex Quaternary alluvial deposits from the Santa Ana River basin, intermixed with older Tertiary sedimentary formations of the Puente and Fernando groups. This geological setting, combined with stringent regulations from the City of Fullerton and compliance with the California Building Code (CBC), which adopts the International Building Code (IBC) with state-specific amendments, demands a precise understanding of soil and bedrock properties. A comprehensive investigation program using surface geophysics is often the critical first step, seamlessly integrated with targeted in-situ testing to calibrate findings and mitigate risks of differential settlement, liquefaction, and slope instability.

Our methodology adheres strictly to American Society for Testing and Materials (ASTM) International standards, ensuring data integrity and regulatory acceptance. Techniques such as Multi-channel Analysis of Surface Waves (MASW) and Electrical Resistivity Tomography (ERT) are deployed to profile shear-wave velocities (Vs) and stratigraphy, which are crucial for seismic site classification per ASCE 7 and the CBC. These methods are not standalone; their value is fully realized when correlated with direct measurements. For instance, geophysical anomalies are ground-truthed using the Standard Penetration Test (SPT) to determine soil density, and the Flat Dilatometer Test (DMT) to obtain high-resolution data on lateral stress and consolidation history. This combined approach provides a robust, defensible geotechnical model for Fullerton's alluvial environment.

In Fullerton’s dynamic development landscape, these geophysical and geotechnical services are pivotal for a spectrum of projects. For the redevelopment of commercial corridors and the construction of new mixed-use structures on variable alluvium, a detailed soil mechanics study is mandatory to design deep foundations that bypass compressible layers. Infrastructure projects, such as the city's ongoing utility upgrades and transportation improvements, rely on accurate subsurface profiling to avoid unforeseen obstructions and assess trench stability. Furthermore, for hillside developments on the Puente Hills' sedimentary formations, integrating geophysics with a plate load test (PLT) is critical to verify bearing capacity and minimize the risk of settlement in potential fill areas.

The deliverable is a fully integrated geotechnical report that transcends simple data compilation. We synthesize geophysical profiles with empirical data from SPT, DMT, and, where required, advanced laboratory analysis such as the oedometer consolidation test on undisturbed samples. This process generates precise design parameters—from seismic site class and allowable bearing pressures to estimated total and differential settlement. The ultimate value is a defensible, code-compliant site characterization that provides engineers and developers in Fullerton with the certainty needed to optimize foundation design, mitigate geohazards, and navigate the local permitting process with confidence.
